Can an AI agent rebuild the reasoning behind a published result?

The paper uses end-to-end reproduction of astronomy studies as a measuring instrument. When a reproduction fails, it asks whether the cause is the agent, an under-specified paper, or information the paper already contains but the agent never connected.

The argument in six steps

  1. Genuinely new scientific claims have no immediate ground truth, so comparing AI systems on discovery is intractable.
  2. Reproduction supplies a known target. The agent must rebuild data selection, method and computation by itself.
  3. Papers are written for expert readers. Omissions that an expert fills from convention become forks for an agent, and each fork can move the number.
  4. A first reproduction of Rix et al. (2022) recovered the qualitative result but not the numbers. The authors first suspected their own pipeline.
  5. After the pipeline was refined, 11 of 13 Nature papers still showed at least one issue that blocked a uniquely specified path.
  6. Twelve predefined paths on Rix et al. span 2.16 to 3.53 kpc and one gives about 2.70 kpc. The decisive +0.02 mas correction was already in the text, but the agents had not linked it to the fit. Matching a number is therefore weak evidence that reasoning was reconstructed.

Machine learning and AI components

What the AI actually does in this study

There is no training, fine-tuning or new model. The ML content is inference-time use of large language models inside an agent loop, plus a design for auditing their outputs.

Paper Frontier instruction-following LLMs serve as reasoning engines. Every analysis used at least two independent foundation models. The agents can retrieve literature, query public archives, execute sandboxed Python and keep a persistent filesystem. Model names, versions, temperature, seeds and run counts are placeholders in this version of the manuscript.

Why a language model is the right test subject

Reading comprehension at scale

A paper is natural language plus equations, tables and figure captions. An LLM is the only general tool that can parse all of it and turn it into code, which makes it a stand-in for the human reader who must reconstruct the method.

Tool use closes the gap to execution

A raw model cannot download Gaia data or run a fit. Wrapping it in an agent loop lets reasoning become queries, scripts and plots, so failures can be traced to concrete steps.

Context is the bottleneck for linking

Critique The central failure is a long-range dependency: a value stated in one section must govern a computation described in another. Chunked reading and lossy structured notes are plausible mechanisms, yet the paper does not test them.

Multiple models guard against one architecture's habits

Cross-model audits make conclusions less dependent on a single model. Critique They only help if errors are independent, and frontier models share much training data.

What the paper does not say

  • Which models were run, in which role, with what temperature, seeds and number of independent runs.
  • Whether any model saw Rix et al. (2022) in training, or how this was ruled out.
  • How the PDF text was extracted, and whether figures and tables were read as images.
  • Any quantitative score for an agent, such as success rate, agreement or calibration. The evaluation is qualitative.

How the roles interact

Paper What is stated

  • Roles are scheduled by Hermes and share tools and a persistent filesystem.
  • The verifier re-derives results independently and checks them against the paper.
  • The adjudicator settles execution versus verification disagreements through cross-model audit, not self-confirmation.
  • Parameters carry provenance, and failure modes and uncertainty must be reported.

Critique What is left open

  • Message format and turn-taking between roles.
  • Whether the verifier sees the coder's reasoning or only its outputs.
  • Retry limits and stopping rules.
  • Whether the planner or a human enumerated the alternative interpretations in the twelve-path study.

From a paper to a defensible judgment

Four views of the same method: the five-stage pipeline, the mismatch-tracing loop used on Rix et al., the attribution logic, and a single diagram joining them.

A. The five-stage pipeline Fig. 1

The agent does not summarise the paper. It retraces the chain of evidence. Input is paper plus public data, the process is auditable (steps, evidence, gaps) and the output is a defensible judgment.

B. The mismatch-tracing loop on Rix et al. Fig. 3

The loop is the method: a mismatch is investigated, not averaged away. On a mismatch the agents trace the earliest unsupported assumption and resume from the stage it affects.

Loop rule. Mismatch found at stage 04 → trace the earliest unsupported assumption (stages 01 to 04) → restart from that stage. Parameters are not chosen by how closely they reproduce the published number. An unresolved mismatch is recorded as unresolved.

C. Attribution logic Fig. 2 and Fig. 7

Every observed difference between reproduction and publication is traced to one of three suspects. Controlled comparisons change one family of variables and hold the others fixed.

Corpus evidence

Eleven of thirteen Nature papers hid a fork

Each paper was processed in two rounds driven by different foundation models. An adjudicator reconciled the records and a human domain expert reviewed every retained item.

How a paper was classified §3.4

A paper lacked a uniquely specified reproduction path when at least one methodological, configurational or interpretational ambiguity forced an independent agent to make a choice the paper did not uniquely determine. Categories overlap, so the denominator is always 13. Items that could not be resolved (for example, suspected text-extraction artefacts) were recorded as unverified, not confirmed.

Eligibility screen §4

Public data. Enough concrete material to define an end-to-end task. A headline conclusion broadly accepted by the field. These criteria concern feasibility and a stable reference, and are said not to pre-select papers by reproduction outcome.

Critique What 11 of 13 does and does not say. It says that for an agent restricted to public text, almost every paper left at least one choice open. It does not say how many of those choices change a result (only one case is quantified, and that case is the ApJ paper, not one of the thirteen). It also mixes two sources of "ambiguity" the framework claims to separate: genuine under-specification and agent-side reading limits such as corrupted equation extraction or incomplete access to Extended Data.

The printed appendix lists clues for all thirteen papers, yet the text counts eleven. It does not mark which two papers were complete or which items were unverified, so the headline cannot be rebuilt from the manuscript alone. A machine-readable audit table is promised as supplementary material.

In-depth case study

Rix et al. (2022): twelve paths through one paper

“The Poor Old Heart of the Milky Way” (ApJ 941, 45). Every agent run recovered the qualitative result: metal-poor stars concentrate toward the Galactic centre and differ from the other population in chemistry and orbits. The numbers did not line up.

Two first anomalies Fig. 4 and Fig. 5

Sample size does not match

Following the stated method gave nearly 30,000 stars, while the published analysis used roughly 18,000 to 20,000. The counting convention could not be reconstructed from the text and public data.

The headline scale was not uniquely determined

The published $\sigma_{\mathrm{RGC}}$ is about 2.7 kpc. The public method left three choices open: which stars count, which sky region, and whether to apply a +0.02 mas parallax correction. The mismatch was recorded as unresolved and parameters were not tuned toward 2.7.

Critique Of the three sample definitions later used, none has N in the published 18,000 to 20,000 range. The path that recovers 2.70 kpc uses about 30,000 stars. The match in σ and the mismatch in N are both unexplained.

Design of the controlled study §3.5, §4.3

Sample definition (3)

Metal-poor cut only; plus parallax S/N ≥ 5; plus S/N ≥ 5 and RUWE < 1.4.

Sky mask (2)

Full sky, or exclude the Galactic plane with |b| > 5°.

Parallax zero-point (2)

No correction, or +0.02 mas added to the observed parallax.

Paper The published value was never an optimisation target, selection criterion or stopping condition. Path labels, parameter grids and stopping rules were fixed before any run. No path was added, removed or re-weighted after the published number became visible to the team.

What moves the answer

Main effects computed from the twelve values (balanced design, so level means are comparable).

Critique Reading of the interaction. The zero-point shifts $\sigma_{\mathrm{RGC}}$ by about 0.83 kpc for the metal-poor sample but only about 0.03 kpc once a parallax S/N cut is applied. The S/N cut keeps stars with larger parallaxes, which are nearer and less sensitive to a 0.02 mas offset, and it also removes distant stars, which shrinks the apparent scale. This explanation is mine; the paper reports the effect without a mechanism.

The +0.02 mas moment §4.3

What the paper says happened

  • All twelve paths ran, so the real question became why any one path deserves preference.
  • Rix et al. had already reported +0.02 mas and cited Lindegren et al. (2021) on the Gaia EDR3 zero-point.
  • What had been compressed was the judgment: why a constant is adequate for this sample, and when it needs re-estimation.
  • The system parsed the number and ran the fit, but did not infer that it should govern the analysis. It connected the pieces only after the experiment made the effect visible.

Critique What to push on

  • If the information was in the text, the first failure was agent-side, which sits awkwardly beside the "papers are under-specified" headline.
  • The zero-point was already one of the three factors when the design was written, after the mismatch was known. Who identified it as a factor is not stated.
  • No ablation, such as a prompt asking about calibration corrections, separates a reasoning limit from a note-taking or prompting limit.
  • The matching path also happens to be the most literal reading of the stated method.

Reproduction loop Reconstructed from Fig. 1, 3

# pseudocode is mine; steps follow the paper's figures
notes  = Reader(paper, supplement)
graph  = Planner(notes)            # tasks, anchors, criteria
env    = prepare(public_data, pinned_packages)
for task in graph:
    out = Coder(task, env)         # log provenance
    ok  = Verifier(out, anchors)
    while not ok:
        stage = trace_earliest_unsupported_assumption()
        if unresolved(stage):
            record_unresolved(stage)   # do not tune
            break
        out = rerun_from(stage)
        ok  = Verifier(out, anchors)
verdict = Adjudicator(records_from_two_models)
return human_review(verdict)

A tension at the centre

The abstract stresses source underspecification: eleven of thirteen papers could not be reproduced along a unique path. For the one case studied in depth, though, the decisive number was stated and cited in the paper. That makes the first failure partly an agent failure, and the framework's own distinction (agent error, underspecification, failure to connect) puts the case in the third bin. The two headline claims describe different phenomena, and the paper would be sharper if it said so.
