Part I. Motivation and framework pp. 1 to 4

Why the question matters, how reproduction is used to evaluate agents, and the shape of the workflow.

Page1

Title, abstract and the central question

AbstractTitle page, authors and abstractPDF page 1 of 31

In one lineThe paper asks whether an AI agent can reconstruct how scientists arrived at a published result, rather than only follow the steps a paper writes down.

Reading the title

Reconstructing
Recovering something that exists but is not fully documented.
Implicit scientific knowledge
Knowledge that shapes methodological decisions but is not necessarily spelled out in a paper.
LLM agents
Large language models equipped with tools: they can search the literature, retrieve datasets, write and run code, and evaluate the output.
End-to-end reproduction
Attempting a whole study from its published description and public data, instead of one isolated calculation.

The problem

A paper states its research questions, equations, data sources, statistical methods, computational procedures and results. It does not state every decision taken on the way. The abstract puts the consequence plainly: a failure to reproduce a result may reflect a limitation of the agent or an underspecification in the source.

Illustration Suppose a paper says: “We selected stars using Gaia EDR3 data and fitted their spatial distribution.” Several questions stay open. Which catalogue exactly? Which quality cuts? Were duplicates removed? Was a parallax correction applied? Were stars near the Galactic plane excluded? Which fitting procedure and priors? An experienced astronomer may supply the conventional answers. An agent may choose differently, run a perfectly valid analysis and still obtain a different number.

What the abstract reports

  • The corpus is fourteen astronomy studies: one case study from The Astrophysical Journal and thirteen papers from Nature.
  • Eleven of the thirteen Nature papers contained an ambiguity that prevented a uniquely specified reproduction path ($11/13\approx84.6\%$).
  • For the case study, twelve predefined paths ($3\times2\times2$: sample definition, sky mask, parallax zero-point treatment) gave $2.16$ to $3.53\kpc$ for the same quantity. Only one recovered the published value, about $2.70\kpc$.
  • The published value was never an optimisation target, selection criterion or stopping condition. The matching path was identified only after all twelve had run.
  • The decisive information, a $+0.02\mas$ parallax zero-point correction, was already in the paper. The agents did not recognise its causal relevance until the analysis made the effect visible.
Demonstrated in the paper
  • In this staged corpus, 11 of 13 Nature papers were classified as having at least one ambiguity, after two model-driven rounds and human review.
  • For one quantity in one paper, twelve predefined paths span $2.16$ to $3.53\kpc$ and exactly one recovers about $2.70\kpc$.
  • The $+0.02\mas$ correction was in the source text, and the agents did not link it to the fit until the experiment made its effect visible.
Suggested or hypothesised
  • Reproduction can be an entry point for finding knowledge hidden in existing research (p. 10).
  • Reproducing hundreds or thousands of papers may reveal patterns that transfer across papers and disciplines (p. 16).
  • The mechanism “present but unlinked” may be general. The authors call it “suggestive” and say broader generalisation needs larger corpora (p. 15).

Paper “The bottleneck is as often a failure to connect relevant information as to retrieve it.” Retrieving a fact and understanding its scientific relevance are different abilities, and a matching number does not show that the second was exercised.

Introduction: why the question matters

§1No figurePDF page 2 of 31

In one lineSingle scientific tasks are within reach of LLM agents; the open question is whether an agent can rebuild the whole chain of reasoning behind a published result.

1. Agents already perform pieces of scientific work

LLMs can assist with literature retrieval, code generation, data analysis, mathematical reasoning and the interpretation of results (Vaswani et al., 2017; Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023). Agentic systems add tool use: they invoke tools, execute programs, retrieve datasets and revise their analyses (Yao et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023).

The paper turns this into a sharper question than whether a model can write plausible scientific text:

Can an AI agent reconstruct the complete reasoning process required to obtain a published scientific result from the information available to it?Wang et al. (2026), §1

2. Generating text is not reconstructing reasoning

Illustration An AI could write “The galaxy distribution exhibits anisotropy on large scales.” The sentence may be plausible. Reconstructing the claim requires the galaxy catalogue, the selection function, the angular distribution, the statistical estimator, the null hypothesis and the significance calculation. These are different levels of capability, and the paper evaluates the second.

3. Why reproduction is a useful test

Novel scientific claims often lack an immediate ground truth. In reproduction the target result already exists, while the agent must independently reconstruct the data, methodological and computational steps that lead to it (Peng, 2011; Stodden et al., 2014; Baker, 2016).

4. The catch: papers are not executable specifications

Papers describe results and methods for human readers who hold background knowledge, disciplinary conventions and practical experience. A paper may state a parameter explicitly while leaving implicit the rationale for applying it to a particular dataset or downstream calculation. Several plausible choices may remain compatible with one published description. For a human expert the omission is largely transparent. For an agent, the same omission can generate several executable interpretations with different quantitative consequences.

Source of failureMeaning
Agentic errorThe AI makes an incorrect decision or calculation.
Source underspecificationThe paper does not provide enough information to determine the method uniquely.
Failure to connect informationThe necessary information exists, but the AI does not recognise its relevance.

What the paper therefore does

Instead of scoring agents on a final number alone, the authors test whether agents can reconstruct the methodological dependencies that link observations, assumptions, parameters, computational procedures and conclusions. Astronomy is the testbed because it combines public datasets, computational analyses, quantitative models and heterogeneous methodological choices. The corpus is fourteen studies, and the workflow separates execution from verification while tracking intermediate artefacts and uncertainty.

Critique A reproduction failure should not be read automatically as evidence that a model cannot reason scientifically. The third source of failure becomes the central finding of the paper, and it is the one that a simple pass or fail score cannot detect.

Evaluation framework

§2Leads into Fig. 1 on p. 4PDF page 3 of 31

In one lineReproduction turns reasoning that has no immediate feedback into a verifiable and comparable pipeline.

The ground-truth problem

A genuinely new hypothesis may take months, years or decades to validate. Even when an AI produces a coherent and plausible conclusion, validation at the time is frequently impossible, so it cannot be told whether the output is a breakthrough, a conjecture or an error. Without immediate ground truth, deciding whether Model A outperforms Model B in scientific exploration becomes intractable, and that blocks iterative improvement.

Background In supervised machine learning, labelled data supply the answer: an image of a galaxy carries the label “spiral”, and a prediction can be checked against it. A new physical theory has no such label.

The reframing

Rather than asking an AI to produce a novel discovery from scratch, the authors test whether it can trace a scientific inquiry end to end: formulating a question, sourcing data, understanding methods, executing analyses, interpreting outcomes and recovering published conclusions. The reproduction protocol converts a reasoning process that lacks immediate feedback into a verifiable and comparative pipeline.

Three controllable dimensions

DimensionWhat it isWhy it is varied
Foundation modelThe LLM that reasons.Cross-model comparison mitigates architectural biases of any single model.
Agent workflowHow the task is divided and executed: retrieval, planning, coding, verification.Separates workflow defects from model limits.
Target publicationThe study being reproduced.Shows whether a failure belongs to the paper or to the system.

If one agent fails on one paper, it does not follow that all LLMs would fail. The model may be weak, the workflow defective or the paper underspecified, and varying the three dimensions is how these are told apart.

A raw generative model cannot autonomously run an end-to-end scientific workflow, so the paper deploys a multi-agent architecture that turns reasoning and coding into concrete actions: literature searches, data acquisition, tool invocation, execution and output validation (Boiko, MacKnight and Gomes, 2023).

Separating execution from adjudication

Agents tend to validate their own outputs too readily. To counter this, the pipeline decouples execution from adjudication, and adds these safeguards:

  • cross-model audits at critical milestones;
  • preservation of intermediate artefacts;
  • provenance tracking for parameters;
  • execution auditing;
  • mandatory reporting of uncertainty and failure modes.

The last sentence of the section records a change of focus. As infrastructure-level errors were isolated and resolved, attention moved from the limits of the problem-solving systems to the information constraints in the source material itself.

Critique The design aims at an auditable process, not a convincing final answer. This matters because an AI can produce a fluent explanation for an incorrect result; preserved code, parameters, provenance and logs let that be checked.

The aim is not to force the agent to obtain the original number. Foundation models, agents, tools and the audit protocol

§3.1 to §3.4No figurePDF page 5 of 31

In one lineTwo or more independent models, five specialised roles, a logged toolchain, and a two-round audit with human review.

The section opens by saying that exact model identifiers, run logs, prompts and per-path configurations are released with the code, and that any value finalised at submission is marked in the text.

3.1 Foundation models and configuration

Several frontier instruction-following LLMs served as reasoning engines. Every analysis was run with at least two independent foundation models, so that no conclusion rests on one architecture. Identifiers, versions, access dates, temperature, top-p, context length, seeds and the number of runs per paper are marked to be completed at submission. In the controlled case study, no model had access to the published numerical target during path generation.

Two independent models reaching the same ambiguity is evidence that the issue is not an isolated model error. Agreement is not proof of correctness, which is why human review is added.

3.2 Agent architecture and roles

The workflow runs on Hermes, an open-source multi-agent framework (Nous Research, 2026) that schedules specialised roles and supplies them with tools and a persistent filesystem.

RoleWhat the paper says it does
ReaderParses the target paper and accessible supplementary material into structured notes.
PlannerDecomposes the reproduction into an executable task graph.
CoderWrites and executes the analysis.
VerifierIndependently re-derives and checks outputs against the paper.
AdjudicatorResolves execution–verification disagreements through cross-model audit rather than self-confirmation.

3.3 Toolchain and execution environment

  • Web and scholarly literature retrieval.
  • Public astronomical archives: the Gaia archive and, where relevant, MAST, TESS and survey releases.
  • A persistent filesystem, so intermediate outputs survive between steps.
  • A sandboxed Python environment with pinned package versions, and shell access.
  • An isolated working directory and software environment for each target paper; every dataset, script, intermediate output and parameter decision is logged with provenance.

3.4 Audit protocol for the thirteen Nature papers

Each paper was processed independently in two rounds driven by different foundation models. The adjudicator reconciled the two records, and a human domain expert reviewed every retained item.

A paper was classified as lacking a uniquely specified reproduction path when at least one methodological, configurational or interpretational ambiguity stopped an independent agent from going from the public materials to the reported numerical result without making a choice the paper did not determine. Issue categories overlap, so the denominator is always the number of papers ($n=13$) and category counts can sum to more than thirteen. Items that could not be resolved, for example because they appeared to come from text extraction, were recorded as unverified.

Critique The manuscript asks for the study itself to be reproducible, yet two placeholders on this page remain open: “to be completed at submission: model names/versions, temperature, top-p, context length, seeds, number of runs, run dates” (§3.1) and “GitHub/Zenodo DOI and environment lockfile” (§3.3). The text alone therefore does not allow the authors’ own experiments to be reconstructed.

What “full factorial” means

Every combination of levels is run. Each of the three sample definitions is paired with each of the two sky masks and each of the two zero-point treatments, giving four paths per sample and twelve in all. Because the grid is complete, the effect of one choice can be read while the other two are held fixed.

The rule that makes it a sensitivity analysis

The twelve paths constitute a sensitivity analysis, not an attempt to reverse-engineer the authors’ choices: the published value (≈2.7 kpc) was not used as an optimization target, a selection criterion, or a stopping condition during path generation, and the single path that matched the published value was identified only after all twelve predefined paths had been executed.Wang et al. (2026), §3.5

Background Tuning parameters until a known answer appears is a form of reverse engineering and invites confirmation bias. A sensitivity analysis asks instead how far the result moves across the plausible choices, which is a property of the method and not of the desired answer.

The corpus and the initial ApJ case

§4, §4.1Fig. 2PDF page 7 of 31

In one lineThe study began with one ApJ paper that exposed the problem, then widened to thirteen Nature papers to test whether the pattern recurs.

Papers were drawn from leading journals not because reproducing them is an end in itself, but because they provide peer-reviewed questions, methodological descriptions and quantitative conclusions that act as fixed reference points.

Three eligibility criteria

1Public data

The underlying data had to be publicly accessible.

2A definable task

The report had to present enough concrete material to define a meaningful end-to-end reproduction from the public material.

3A settled headline

The headline conclusion had to be broadly accepted, so that a discrepancy could not simply be blamed on contested science.

These criteria concern feasibility and the existence of a stable reference. They are not about whether a paper could be reproduced, and so, the paper argues, they do not pre-select papers by result.

4.1 The initial ApJ case

The first paper is “The Poor Old Heart of the Milky Way” by Rix et al. (The Astrophysical Journal, 2022). Using Hermes, the authors connected several LLMs and ran multiple independent reproduction attempts. Every run recovered the central qualitative result: metal-poor stars are concentrated toward the Galactic centre, and the two stellar populations differ clearly in chemical composition and orbital properties.

At the quantitative level, repeated runs did not align with the reported values. The agents began tracing the discrepancies backwards through the workflow, which is the subject of Figure 3 on the next page.

Background A metal-poor star has a low abundance of elements heavier than helium. The notation $\MH$ expresses this on a logarithmic scale relative to the Sun, so that $\MH\lt-1.5$ selects stars far poorer in heavy elements than the Sun. Such stars are typically old, which fits the paper’s title.

Figure 3: the workflow used on Rix et al.

The agents worked through six stages: inventory, cross-match, reproduce, compare, diagnose and review. A mismatch is traced to the earliest unsupported assumption, and the workflow restarts from the stage that was affected. The figure carries its own summary: the loop is the method, and a mismatch is investigated, not averaged away.

Figure 4: one stated cut does not reproduce the sample size

Following the method stated in the paper gave nearly 30,000 stars, whereas the published analysis used approximately 18,000 to 20,000. From the paper text and the public data available now, the authors could not reconstruct the same counting convention.

In the figure, the stated cut $\MH\lt-1.5$ gives 30,589 unique stars in the Zenodo table. An explicit ladder gives 15,063 with Zenodo kinematics and 13,141 after a parallax cut below $0.25\mas$. The figure’s own reading is that an unstated subsample or counting convention is likely, and that the shape of the metallicity distribution was tested separately.

Four possible explanations

Unstated subsample
An additional definition of which stars belong to the analysed set.
Quality cuts
Catalogues hold measurements of unequal reliability, so researchers remove those that fail quality requirements. Unreported cuts change the sample.
Deduplication
A catalogue can hold repeated or overlapping records, and a rule is needed to decide which are unique sources.
Data version
Different releases of a survey can hold different measurements or corrections.

Background The sample is not a technical detail. If additional stars from different spatial regions enter the sample, the estimate of the population’s radial extent can change, so the selection feeds directly into the scientific result.

The quantity

The central spatial-scale parameter is, in the paper’s words, the radial dispersion of the metal-poor population, denoted $\sigma_{\mathrm{RGC}}$. The paper reports about $2.7\kpc$. One kiloparsec is about 3,262 light years, so this is roughly 8,800 light years.

Changing parameters until the output agrees with the publication, without an independent justification, would risk confirmation bias. Recording the mismatch as open is the more defensible practice.

Why the source of the failure was unclear

The first failure could not be located precisely. It might reflect genuinely missing practical information in the paper, which would be a potentially important scientific clue. It could as easily come from the authors’ own lack of domain expertise, a hidden defect in the agent pipeline, or a failure in the model–tool chain. The authors therefore set a new goal: reproduce a small group of papers from the same broad field while refining the workflow and reducing interference from pipeline, agents, models and human operation.

§4.2 begins: expanding to thirteen Nature papers

When the first paper could not be reproduced exactly, the agent system was the primary suspect. As the pipeline was refined, attention shifted from the system solving the problem to the information the problem supplied. The authors selected thirteen astrophysics papers from Nature and ran a batch reproduction. Eleven contained at least one issue that blocked a uniquely specified path; only two were complete enough, within the scope of the tasks, to support an independent path from data to the target number. From verification to discovery

The authors first hoped to use reproduction as an indirect test of whether AI can perform scientific discovery. The batch experiment suggested it might be more: an entry point for finding new knowledge. When an agent retraces the path from data to conclusion, research judgements that were omitted or treated as obvious can become visible again.

A discrepancy between a reproduced result and a published result is not necessarily an error to be eliminated. Once verified and explained, it may reveal a relationship among the source data, the analytical method, and the scientific conclusion that has not been made fully explicit.Wang et al. (2026), §4.2

§4.3 begins: returning to the first paper

With this idea, the authors returned to the first paper using the revised system. They stopped tuning toward $2.7\kpc$ and asked a different question: if the paper does not uniquely specify an analysis path, how much can scientifically plausible choices change the conclusion? Three choices were not uniquely determined: which stars to include, whether a systematic parallax offset should be corrected, and whether regions near the Galactic plane should be excluded. The twelve paths “were not twelve guesses about what the authors had actually done”; they were a systematic exploration of the consequences of choices the paper did not reduce to one executable specification.

Critique What 11 of 13 does and does not say. It says that, for an agent limited to public text, almost every paper left at least one choice open. It does not say how many of those choices change a result, because only the ApJ case is quantified. It also groups genuine underspecification with agent-side reading limits such as corrupted equation extraction. The appendix lists clues for all thirteen papers while the text counts eleven, and it does not mark which two were complete. See The Nature audit.

The sensitivity analysis and its twelve results

What a sensitivity analysis is

It investigates how the output of a model changes when inputs or assumptions change. For the case study the output is one number, as a function of the three choices:

$$Y = f(X_1, X_2, X_3)$$

Here $Y$ is $\sigma_{\mathrm{RGC}}$, $X_1$ is the sample definition, $X_2$ the sky mask and $X_3$ the parallax treatment. Varying the inputs systematically shows which assumptions have most influence.

The twelve results

All twelve paths converged and executed successfully, giving spatial scales from $2.16$ to $3.53\kpc$. One path, with a particular sample definition, a parallax zero-point correction and the full-sky region, gave about $2.70\kpc$, closely matching the reported result.

Foreclosing a reverse-engineering reading

The paper states that the published value was not an optimisation target, selection criterion or stopping condition. Path labels, parameter grids and stopping rules were fixed before any path ran, and no path was added, removed or re-weighted after the published number became visible to the analysis team.

Critique The manuscript says cross-model comparisons were used throughout and names two models as examples in Fig. 7, but it reports no per-model or per-workflow results. Families A and B cannot be checked from the text, and the twelve-path study is the only fully reported variation. See Workflows.

What the authors say happened

The primary concern was not which path came closest to $2.7\kpc$, but why, given that all twelve executed, any single path should be preferred over the other eleven. At this point the AI surfaced a connection it had previously failed to establish. The information was not absent: Rix et al. had already reported $+0.02\mas$ and cited Lindegren et al. (2021) on the Gaia EDR3 parallax zero-point.

What had been compressed, the paper says, was the methodological judgment behind the parameter: why a constant approximation remained appropriate for this specific sample, under what conditions it held, and when it required re-estimation for later data releases or alternative samples (Lindegren et al., 2021; ESA, 2021). That rationale did not reappear as an explicit step in the quantitative method.

The system “successfully parsed ‘+0.02 mas’ and executed the subsequent fitting procedure”, but it did not infer that a value introduced earlier in the text needed to govern the quantitative analysis, or that omitting it would materially alter the outcome. Only after the twelve-path experiment made the effect quantitatively visible did the system connect information spread across different sections. The salient finding, in the authors’ words, is that the parameter was not missing; its causal relevance was not recognised.

Information retrieval

Finding a parameter in a document.

Scientific reasoning

Understanding when, why and how that parameter must enter a calculation. The authors argue that this is the harder problem.

The physics behind the correction

For a parallax $\varpi$ in milliarcseconds the distance is $d\,(\mathrm{pc})=1000/\varpi$. Gaia parallaxes carry a systematic bias that depends on magnitude, colour and position (Lindegren et al., 2021). A zero-point correction removes the average bias, and a small additive change in $\varpi$ shifts every inferred distance.

Illustration A measured $\varpi=0.50\mas$ gives $d=2000\,\mathrm{pc}$. If the corrected parallax is $0.52\mas$, then $d=1000/0.52\approx1923\,\mathrm{pc}$, a difference of about 77 pc. The example is simplified: real Gaia corrections depend on the observational properties of each star, and the actual analysis fits a spatial distribution to a whole stellar sample.

Illustration Imagine a paper that introduces a calibration offset early on, describes a dataset next, fits a statistical model later and reports an estimate at the end. Does this make published conclusions unreliable?

No, the paper says. A paper must prioritise novelty, and many choices about parameters, data filtering, calibration and uncertainty handling are not novel, yet they cannot be made incorrectly without consequence. Discussion and conclusion

§8Ends the main textPDF page 16 of 31

In one linePapers preserve what scientists found, but not always how they knew a choice was right; large-scale reproduction is proposed as a way to recover that.

The conclusion cites Yann LeCun’s remark at VivaTech in 2023 that much of human knowledge is non-linguistic and has not been captured by current AI. The paper applies the idea to science: papers preserve what scientists discovered, but not always the full process by which they knew that a particular choice was right. This may be an easily overlooked risk when AI attempts to conduct discovery independently.

The authors argue that end-to-end reproduction can serve as a reproducibility test and also as an evaluation framework for implicit scientific knowledge and reasoning in AI. In the Rix case a predefined sensitivity analysis, never used for tuning, shows that a numerical match is “neither necessary evidence of correct reasoning nor a stable target to optimize toward”, whereas the failure to connect a correction already in the paper to the downstream fit is directly observable.

What the authors propose next

The goal is not simply to reproduce more papers, but to enable models, through large-scale reproduction, to reconstruct knowledge that is embedded in scientific practice and compressed in publication. As the scale grows from dozens of papers to hundreds or thousands, recurring parameter conflicts, methodological variations and failure modes may reveal patterns that transfer across papers and disciplines.

Paper “We do not claim that reproduction has produced new scientific discoveries here; rather, we show that it can serve as a disciplined entry point through which such implicit knowledge, and, potentially, new scientific questions, may come to light.”

Why the sampler matters

In Bayesian estimation with Markov chain Monte Carlo the posterior is

$$p(\theta\mid D)\propto p(D\mid\theta)\,p(\theta)$$

with $\theta$ the model parameters, $D$ the data, $p(D\mid\theta)$ the likelihood and $p(\theta)$ the prior. Different samplers, likelihood implementations and convergence criteria give different practical results, so saying that MCMC was used does not guarantee a numerical reproduction.

LessonReproducibility needs a clear split between observational inputs, calculated outputs and the acceptable numerical difference.

Paper 12: star formation near the Sun and the Local Bubble

Appendix A, no. 12Milky Way · star formation · Nature (printed as 2021)PDF page 27 of 31

In one lineThe clues concern a qualitative claim with no metric, an idealised model whose exponent was inferred, and missing numerical content.

Zucker et al. (2022). Star formation near the Sun is driven by expansion of the Local Bubble. Nature 601, 334–337. DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04286-5.

The appendix table prints 2021; the reference list gives 2022.

The science in brief

The study reports that star formation near the Sun is driven by the expansion of the Local Bubble, a large low-density cavity in the interstellar medium around the Sun, shaped by past stellar activity.

What the audit recorded

  • The claimed “qualitative morphology” has no metric or threshold. The expansion model is idealised, and the exponent of its equation was inferred backwards from a standard form in an external citation. Neither interpretation gives a numerical reproduction tolerance.
  • The specific variant of the Milky Way-like gravitational potential and its integration settings are unspecified. External numerical content, including the cluster catalogue, ages and dust geometry, is missing.

How the audit surfaced the clues

The audit enumerated three issues: the potential variant, the reproduction tolerance and the formula exponent.

How would an agent decide that a reproduced morphology is similar enough? Some defined comparison procedure is needed. The gravitational potential also controls the motion of stars and clusters, so independent simulations with different settings can give different trajectories.

LessonEven a largely qualitative astrophysical study can rest on substantial hidden numerical modelling decisions.

Paper 13: a dormant overmassive black hole in the early Universe

Appendix A, no. 13JWST · early black holes · Nature 2024PDF page 28 of 31

In one lineThe clues concern ambiguous units, uncertain access and a corrected-versus-uncorrected luminosity chain.

Juodžbalis et al. (2024). A dormant overmassive black hole in the early Universe. Nature 636, 594–597. DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08210-5.

The science in brief

The study identifies a dormant, overmassive black hole in the early Universe, one whose mass is unusually large relative to the stellar mass of its host galaxy. This bears on how supermassive black holes formed and grew early on.

What the audit recorded

  • The units of the black-hole mass are ambiguous, as are those of the host galaxy’s stellar mass.
  • Whether the paper’s actual processing uses a virial workflow remains to be verified. A simple virial estimate is $M\sim R\,\Delta V^2/G$.
  • One interpretation had access to only a limited part of the main text because material access differed, so some discrepancies may reflect source availability and not method.
  • Data described as “expected to be public” are an eligibility expectation, not verified availability.
  • The luminosity of about $10^{45}$ is the “corrected” target, but the corrected-versus-uncorrected chain is ambiguous and is also affected by text-extraction problems.

How the audit surfaced the clues

The audit enumerated four issue classes: unit ambiguity, reproducibility, simulation availability and the luminosity chain.

LessonThe provenance of a measurement matters as much as its value. Propagating an uncorrected luminosity into a mass estimate would give the wrong mass.

Part VI. Putting the paper together Guide

A summary of the argument across all 31 pages, followed by an assessment, a self-test and a suggested route for a discussion.

What the paper contributes

1. An evaluation framework

End-to-end reproduction as a way to test whether agents can reconstruct scientific reasoning, judged on the whole process and not only on the final number.

2. An audit of thirteen papers

Eleven of thirteen Nature papers showed a methodological, configurational or interpretational ambiguity that blocked a uniquely specified path.

3. A controlled experiment

Twelve plausible analysis paths give different values of one quantity, and show that an agent can retrieve a parameter without grasping its causal relevance.

$$\text{Paper}\rightarrow\text{Data}\rightarrow\text{Method}\rightarrow\text{Code}\rightarrow\text{Result}\rightarrow\text{Verification}$$

Five concepts to hold on to

ConceptWhat it means
Scientific reproducibilityReconstructing a published result from the available research materials.
Implicit knowledgeMethodological understanding that a publication does not fully express.
Multi-agent architectureDividing a complex research workflow among specialised AI agents.
Sensitivity analysisStudying how methodological choices change the final output.
Causal relevanceUnderstanding why a particular measurement, correction or assumption must influence a downstream calculation.

Reproducing a number is not reproducing the reasoning

Suppose an agent obtains $\sigma_{\mathrm{RGC}}\approx2.70\kpc$ exactly. That alone does not show it understood the study. It could have used the wrong dataset, applied an unjustified correction, chosen an arbitrary sample or tuned parameters until the target appeared. Numerical agreement cannot establish that the reasoning was reconstructed; the stronger test is whether the agent can explain and justify every methodological dependency. This is the paper’s central intellectual contribution.

Assessment

Strengths
  • The question bears on the future of AI-assisted science.
  • Source ambiguity is separated from agent failure.
  • The twelve-path design is controlled and fixed in advance.
  • Separating execution from verification reduces self-confirmation.
  • The Gaia parallax example gives a concrete case of the central argument.
Weaknesses and open questions
  • The corpus is small: thirteen papers in the audit.
  • The detailed experiment concerns one quantity in one paper.
  • No quantitative benchmark of different models’ reasoning is reported.
  • The count of ambiguities depends partly on human interpretation.
  • Generalisation beyond astronomy is untested.

Critique The manuscript itself has a reproducibility gap. On p. 5 the model identifiers and settings, and the GitHub or Zenodo DOI and the environment lockfile, are marked as to be completed at submission, so the text alone does not let the authors’ own experiments be rebuilt. This is worth raising in discussion, because reproducibility is the subject of the paper. The chapters on Critique and Assumptions develop the points.
